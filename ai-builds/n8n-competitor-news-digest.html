Competitor News Daily Digest in n8n

An n8n workflow that turns a week of news about five AI video companies into one categorised email. Duplicate coverage is merged and irrelevant matches are dropped by one model, then each story is summarised by a lighter one.

Context
Assignment, Airtribe AI-First Product Management
Built
August 2026
Stack
n8n, Serper, Gemini 3.6 Flash and 3.1 Flash-Lite, Google Sheets, Gmail
Running cost
₹0: Serper's free credits (5 per run) and Gemini's free tier
Status
Ran on its daily schedule in August 2026; not running now

The workflow, step by step

  1. 01Start every morning at 8 AMSchedule Trigger

    Fires once a day at 8 AM. Everything else runs from this one trigger.

    Trigger at
    8:00 AM, every day
  2. 02Read the competitor listGoogle Sheets · Get rows

    Reads one row per competitor: name, domain, aliases and a search query tuned for that name. The list lives in a sheet, so I can add a competitor without editing the workflow.

    NameSearch query
    Runway"Runway AI" OR "RunwayML" OR "Runway Gen-4"
    Kling AI"Kling AI" OR "Kling 3.0"
    Luma AI"Luma AI" OR "Luma Labs"
    Pika"Pika AI" OR "Pika Labs"
    Google Veo"Google Veo" OR "Veo 3"
  3. 03Search Google NewsHTTP Request · Serper

    Runs once per competitor and asks Serper's Google News endpoint for up to 10 articles from the past week. Searching each competitor on its own means every result arrives already tagged, and each name gets its own query. That matters because three of the five names are ordinary words: a plain search for “Runway” returns fashion shows and airport runways, and “Pika” returns Pokémon.

    Request
    POST https://google.serper.dev/news
    Body
    {{ JSON.stringify({ q: $json.Query, num: 10, tbs: "qdr:w" }) }}
    Window
    tbs: "qdr:w" is Google's past-week filter
    API key
    Held in n8n's credential store, not in the workflow
  4. 04One item per articleSplit Out

    Serper returns a nested list of articles for each competitor. This turns every article into its own item.

    Field
    news
  5. 05Tag each article with its competitorEdit Fields

    Re-attaches the competitor name from the sheet row and keeps only what the AI steps need.

    Fields kept
    competitor, url, title, description, source, date
  6. 06Remove exact duplicatesRemove Duplicates

    Drops repeated URLs, which appear when one article matches two competitors' searches. The check is exact and free, so it runs before any model sees the data. Different outlets covering the same event are a separate problem, and the next AI step handles that.

    Compare on
    url
  7. 07Gather everything into one batchAggregate

    Collapses all the articles into a single item. Deciding whether two articles describe the same event is a comparison, so the next step has to see every article at once.

    Output field
    articles
  8. 08Cluster stories and judge relevanceBasic LLM Chain · Gemini 3.6 Flash

    Groups articles that cover the same real-world event, lists every tracked competitor each story involves, and marks each story relevant or not. An executive leaving a competitor counts as relevant. A meme made with a competitor's tool, or a listicle that only names it, doesn't.

    Show the prompt
    You are reviewing news articles gathered while tracking AI video generation
competitors. Some are duplicate coverage of the same event from different
outlets. Some mention multiple competitors in one roundup piece. Some
aren't really news about any tracked competitor at all, despite matching
the search.

Articles:
{{ JSON.stringify($json.articles) }}

STEP 1 — Group into distinct real-world stories.

Two articles are the SAME story only if they describe the same specific
event or announcement (e.g., the same funding round, the same launch).
Similar topic or genre is NOT enough — do not merge separate comparison
articles or separate listicles just because they cover similar ground.

Example — SAME story: "Kling 3.0 raises $2.8B" + "Kling captures majority
of July funding" (same funding round, different outlets).
Example — DIFFERENT stories: "Top 10 AI Video Tools" listicle + "Seedance
vs Kling" comparison article (different pieces, just similar topic, even
if both mention the same competitors).

For each story, list every tracked competitor it's genuinely about.

STEP 2 — Judge relevance for each story.

Mark relevant: true if the article is substantively about the tracked
competitor itself — its product, technology, business, funding,
partnerships, or strategy. This includes factual descriptions, reviews,
and comparisons of the product, not just breaking-news events.

Mark relevant: false if any of the following apply:
- The competitor's name isn't actually mentioned (being topically about
  AI video in general is not enough on its own).
- The article is really about something else — a third party's content,
  project, or story — that merely happens to use or mention the
  competitor's tool in passing, rather than being about the competitor
  itself. Example: a meme video "made with" a competitor's tool is about
  the meme, not the competitor — mark false.
- The article is a generic listicle or roundup that mentions the
  competitor's name only as one entry among many, with no real
  substance about that competitor specifically.

One exception: a personnel move (an executive or employee leaving the
competitor) IS relevant, even though the article's nominal subject is
their new venture — it signals something real about the competitor's
talent and strategy. Mark these true.

Return only a JSON object with a "stories" key. For each story, include
every field (url, title, description, source, date) for every article
in that story, not just the URL — the next step needs this content to
write from.

    Model: Gemini 3.6 Flash

    The more capable of the two models. Deciding whether two differently worded articles describe one event is a judgement call, so it gets the stronger model.

    Structured output parser

    Forces the reply into one JSON shape: a list of stories, each with its competitors, its articles and a relevant flag. Auto-fix is on, so a reply that doesn't parse goes to a second model for repair.

    Show the expected shape
    {
  "stories": [
    {
      "competitors": ["Kling AI"],
      "articles": [
        {"url": "https://example.com/a", "title": "...", "description": "...", "source": "...", "date": "..."}
      ],
      "relevant": true
    }
  ]
}

    Auto-fix model: Gemini 3.1 Flash-Lite

    Only called when the main reply fails to parse.

  9. 09One item per storySplit Out

    Unpacks the model's list of stories so each story moves on as its own item.

    Field
    output.stories
  10. 10Keep relevant stories onlyFilter

    Passes stories marked relevant and drops the rest. On the 14 August test run it removed 7 of 13 clustered stories, including an unrelated chip-materials startup that three outlets had covered.

    Condition
    relevant is true
  11. 11Summarise and categorise each storyBasic LLM Chain · Gemini 3.1 Flash-Lite

    Runs once per story. It writes two to three sentences under 300 characters, picks exactly one of eight categories, and chooses the best source link. It may only use facts in the titles and snippets, so a missing amount or name means a shorter summary, not an invented one.

    Show the prompt
    You are writing one entry for a competitor-tracking digest read by a
senior product manager. Be specific: name products, dollar amounts, and
companies exactly. No vague adjectives like "innovative" or "game-changing".

Only use facts actually present in the title/description below. If a
specific detail (dollar amount, investor name, partner name) isn't in the
source text, don't invent one — write a shorter, more general summary
instead of a specific-sounding but fabricated one.

Story (competitors: {{ $json.competitors.join(', ') }}):
{{ JSON.stringify($json.articles) }}

SUMMARY: 2-3 sentences, under 300 characters. Combine the most complete
facts across every article — if one outlet has a number and another names
a person or partner, include both. If the story involves multiple tracked
competitors, make clear how each is involved.

CATEGORY: exactly one of: Product Launch, Funding, Partnership,
Hiring/Leadership, Acquisition, Reviews & Comparisons, Company Update, Other.

Categorize by what the story means for the TRACKED COMPETITOR, not by the
article's nominal subject. Example: an executive leaving the competitor to
found a new company is Hiring/Leadership, not Product Launch.

Funding means the company RAISED outside investment (a round, a named
investor). Revenue, ARR, valuation mentioned without a round, or other
financial metrics are Company Update, not Funding — these are different
even though both involve dollar figures.

Reviews & Comparisons: third-party articles reviewing or comparing the
product against others, with no other news content.

Other: generic descriptions or mentions with no real news value at all.

LINK: copy one URL exactly as it appears in the articles above — prefer the
one with the most specific, sourced detail. Never modify or construct a URL.

Return only a JSON object, no other text:
{"summary": "...", "category": "Funding", "link": "https://..."}

    Model: Gemini 3.1 Flash-Lite

    A lighter model with a higher requests-per-minute allowance on Gemini's free tier. This step fires once per story, and running it on the bigger model hit the rate limit on the first full test. Summarising is closer to formatting than reasoning, because relevance was already decided upstream.

    Structured output parser

    Category is an enum of the eight allowed values, so an invented category fails here instead of breaking the email's grouping.

    Show the schema
    {
  "type": "object",
  "properties": {
    "summary": { "type": "string" },
    "category": {
      "type": "string",
      "enum": ["Product Launch", "Funding", "Partnership", "Hiring/Leadership", "Acquisition", "Reviews & Comparisons", "Company Update", "Other"]
    },
    "link": { "type": "string" }
  },
  "required": ["summary", "category", "link"]
}

    Auto-fix model: Gemini 3.1 Flash-Lite

    Only called when the main reply fails to parse.

  12. 12Re-attach competitors and stamp the dateEdit Fields

    Copies each story's competitor list back from the Filter step, because the model's reply doesn't carry it, and stamps the run date.

    Adds
    competitors, loggedDate
  13. 13Build the emailCode · JavaScript

    Groups stories by category in a fixed order, skips empty categories, and writes the HTML: competitor names in bold, then the summary and a View link.

    Show the code
    const CATEGORY_ORDER = [
  "Product Launch", "Funding", "Partnership", "Acquisition",
  "Hiring/Leadership", "Reviews & Comparisons", "Company Update", "Other"
];

const normalize = s => (s || "").trim().toLowerCase();
const categoryMap = Object.fromEntries(CATEGORY_ORDER.map(c => [normalize(c), c]));

const stories = $input.all().map(item => ({
  ...item.json.output,
  competitors: item.json.competitors
}));

const grouped = {};
for (const story of stories) {
  const matched = categoryMap[normalize(story.category)] || "Other";
  if (!grouped[matched]) grouped[matched] = [];
  grouped[matched].push(story);
}

const dateStr = $now.toFormat('MMM d');
let html = `<h2>Competitor Digest — ${dateStr}</h2>`;

for (const category of CATEGORY_ORDER) {
  if (!grouped[category]) continue;
  html += `<h3>${category}</h3><ul>`;
  for (const story of grouped[category]) {
    const names = story.competitors.join(', ');
    html += `<li><strong>${names}</strong> — ${story.summary} <a href="${story.link}">View</a></li>`;
  }
  html += `</ul>`;
}

return [{ json: { html, subject: `Competitor Digest — ${dateStr}` } }];
  14. 14Send the digestGmail · Send a message

    Sends the HTML to my own inbox. I first planned to deliver to Slack, but the available workspace needed admin approval for scopes this workflow didn't need, so it sends from and to my own Gmail account instead.

    Subject
    Competitor news daily digest

Summary

Problem
Following five AI video competitors means wading through duplicated coverage and noise. Three of the five names are ordinary words, so plain searches return airport runways and Pokémon.
What it does
Sends one email each morning. Duplicate coverage is merged, irrelevant matches are dropped, and each story gets two or three lines, a category and its source link.
How
Seven plain data steps collect and clean the news. Two Gemini models then do the judgement work: the stronger one clusters stories and judges relevance, and the lighter one summarises.

How a run works

Steps 1 to 7 use no AI. They read the competitor list, search each competitor separately, flatten the results and drop exact duplicate URLs. That last check is exact and free, so it runs before any model sees the data.

Steps 8 to 14 handle what string matching can't. The first model sees every article at once. It merges coverage of the same event from different outlets and marks each story relevant or not. Then a second, lighter model writes one entry per surviving story, and plain code groups the entries by category and builds the email.

Both AI steps go through a structured output parser. The summary step's category must be one of eight fixed values, so a made-up category fails at the schema instead of quietly breaking the email.

A real digest

Decisions

  • One search per competitor, not one combined query. A single OR'd query would use fewer credits, but the results would come back untagged. Matching names back into the text fails exactly on the ambiguous names that needed tuned queries. At five competitors a run, the extra credits don't matter.
  • Two duplicate problems, two mechanisms. Identical URLs are removed with an exact check. The same event covered by different outlets goes to the clustering model, because no string comparison catches it.
  • Relevance judged inside clustering. That step already reads every article, so a separate scoring pass would add cost without adding information.
  • Grouped by category, not by competitor. The category list stays at eight however many competitors are tracked. Competitor names stay in bold on every line.
  • The taxonomy was revised after real output. With the first six categories, too much landed in Other, and an article about Luma AI's revenue was filed under Funding even though its point was that Luma has raised nothing. I added Reviews & Comparisons and Company Update, and tightened Funding to mean a raise from outside investors.
  • Each model matched to its job. One call per story on the stronger model hit Gemini's free-tier rate limit on the first full test. I split the work instead of paying for capacity: the stronger model for the comparisons, the lighter one for the per-story formatting.

Limits

  • Summaries are written from titles and snippets, not full articles, so they're only as detailed as the snippet.
  • Grounding is instructed, not enforced. Nothing checks a summary against its source, so treat figures and names as pointers to confirm.
  • Stories repeat across days. The search covers the past week and the workflow doesn't remember what it has already sent, so Kling AI's July funding round appears in both the 14 and 19 August digests. The history log I designed would fix this; it isn't built yet.
  • A quiet day sends no email, which looks the same as a failed run. A branch that sends a short “nothing new” message would close that gap.
  • Some links are Google redirect URLs rather than direct article links.

Get the workflow

Import the file into n8n with Import from file. I've replaced my sheet ID, email address and credential IDs with placeholders. To run it, create four credentials (Serper API Key, Google Gemini, Google Sheets OAuth2 and Gmail OAuth2), point the Sheets step at your own competitor list, and set your own address in the Gmail step.
