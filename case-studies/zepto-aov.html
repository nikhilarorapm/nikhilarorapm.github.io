Zepto AOV: closing the order-value gap with Blinkit

Zepto trails Blinkit by ₹100–120 per order. At 2.3M daily orders, every ₹50 of average order value is worth ₹11.5 Cr in daily GOV.

Summary

Question
Why are Zepto baskets small, and what would make them bigger?
Key finding
Most orders come from top-up and impulse missions, and the ₹149 free-delivery threshold acts as a stopping point, not a spending trigger.
What I proposed
Task-completion recommendations (RICE 14.4), moment bundles (9.4) and a “Fresh Today” perishables experience (3.0) on a six-month roadmap.
90-day target
Overall AOV up 3–5%, and 0.5–1.0 more items per order.

Decomposing AOV

AOV is the average spend per order. Raising it adds revenue from existing users at no acquisition cost, which improves unit economics directly. In the KPI tree, commission is driven by gross order value, and GOV = AOV × number of orders. AOV itself is items per order × average selling price per item, and both levers depend on why people open the app.

Most users fall into the first two behaviours. Replenishment is where AOV jumps 3–5×, but it's a small fraction: most people still see Zepto as the last-minute app, not a daily grocery store.

Five blockers

  • A mission-driven mindset. Users arrive with a one- or two-item list, and the 10-minute promise trains them to minimise time in the app.
  • The free-delivery threshold becomes the goal. The progress bar celebrates reaching ₹149, which signals “you're done”. Someone who might have spent ₹250 stops at ₹155.
  • Habitual reordering narrows categories. “Buy again” re-adds familiar items and the recommendations surface more of the same.
  • Convenience over discovery. Searching “onion” shows onions, not the besan, chillies and oil for the pakoras being made.
  • No trust in perishable quality. Without the touch and feel of a kirana store, users buy vegetables offline, leaving the highest-AOV category underused.

Today's journey runs need, search, reach the threshold, checkout. The goal is need, browse, discover complementary items, add, checkout.

Standard AOV constructs are table stakes across the three apps, so rearranging recommendations yields marginal gains. What's needed are solutions that change user behaviour.

Three solutions

1 · Task-completion recommendations

Instead of generic “People also bought”, recommend what completes the user's task: Maggi with cheese, butter and Coke; bread with jam, peanut butter and eggs; diapers with wipes and rash cream. The widget sits at position 1 on the cart page with a “Forgot something?” tab and time-of-day personalisation, plus a “Running out” predictor: “You purchased cooking oil 28 days ago. Running low?”

Expected lift: if 25–30% of orders add one ₹40–60 item, that's ₹10–18 per order, about 2–3% of blended AOV. Effort is low to moderate on existing infrastructure.

2 · Context-triggered moment bundles

Pre-curated bundles of 4–8 items for a moment, with an Add All button and a 5–10% discount: a Friday movie-night kit, rainy-day chai and pakoda essentials, a paneer butter masala kit. Mini missions add light gamification.

Why it works: framing. “Paneer” sets a ₹100 mental budget; a “Paneer Butter Masala Kit” sets ₹350. Bundle orders run ₹300–450 against ₹150–300 for top-ups, so an 8–12% attach rate moves AOV. Effort is medium, with ongoing merchandising.

3 · “Fresh Today” daily perishables

Fresh produce has the highest natural AOV (5–10 items an order) and the blocker is trust. The fix combines trust markers (sourcing time, freshness grade, local order counts), freshness storytelling on the order-tracking screen instead of generic ads, and habit nudges that are deliberately not discount-led. Subscriptions were ruled out because daily grocery needs variety.

Expected lift: per-user AOV can rise 2–4×, so even 5–10% conversion of monthly users moves the needle. It's a 3–6 month play with supply-chain dependencies.

Reach is the share of Zepto's 4.79 crore annual transacting users each solution touches: 50%, 40% and 20%. Impact runs 1–5, confidence 0.5–1.0 and effort 1–10.

Beyond the scores, Fresh Today depends on consistent produce quality, since one bad batch damages trust for good, so supply-chain readiness comes first. Task-completion recommendations also generate the data (what gets added, by whom, when) that informs the other two.

Metrics and guardrails

KPIs, 90 days

  • Overall AOV up 3–5% from baseline
  • 0.5–1.0 more items per order
  • AOV lift among Top-Uppers and Threshold Optimizers

Guardrails

  • Order frequency: bigger baskets shouldn't come from fewer orders
  • Delivery time: larger baskets mustn't break the 10-minute promise
  • Cart abandonment: widgets shouldn't cause decision fatigue
  • Quality complaints: pause Fresh Today if they spike

Limits of this work

  • Built on secondary research only, with no user interviews or surveys.
  • Segment shares and reach shares are hypotheses, to be validated against Zepto's cart and search analytics.
  • AOV figures for all three apps are estimates.
